## 1. Method

**Scope.** Twelve firms, 2023-01 through 2026-04, grouped as:

- AI-native labs and agent vendors — Anthropic, OpenAI, Cognition, Sierra, Cursor / Anysphere, Replit, Perplexity
- AI-forward operating companies — Shopify, Klarna, Stripe, Palantir
- Consulting firms using their own AI internally and as client offering — McKinsey QuantumBlack, Bain, BCG X (Deloitte referenced comparatively)
- Professional services case (Harvey + A&O Shearman) treated as a cross-cutting signal, not a firm slot

**Evidence sources.** Earnings calls (Klarna Q-letters 2024-02 through 2025-Q3, IPO prospectus March 2025); press releases (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, A&O Shearman, Anthropic); founder / CEO posts on X, LinkedIn, and podcasts (Lütke Shopify memo 2025-04; Masad on Semafor 2025-01 and 2025-05; Srinivas on All-In Podcast 2026-03; Collison on Stripe PR count); tech press (The Information, Bloomberg, CNBC, Fortune, Semafor, TechCrunch, Washington Post, Variety); first-party Cognition blog on Devin's 2025 performance; independent testing (Answer.AI on Devin completion rate); analyst reporting (Contrary Research, Dealroom, PitchBook); engineering labor market data (Fonzi AI / Pragmatic Engineer on FDE growth).

**Evidence hygiene.** Observed (with citation) and inferred (from pattern across sources) are kept separate per section. Confidence is labelled H / M / L. Dates are absolute. Where a firm's announced restructuring and on-the-ground reality differ, the gap is flagged in §5.

**Deliberate exclusions.** Meta's "Superintelligence Labs" 2025 hiring spree, Google DeepMind internal reorganization, and xAI ramp are not covered as firm slots — they are frontier-lab variants of patterns already captured via OpenAI and Anthropic and would dilute the focus on human-AI *collaboration* rather than pure lab restructuring. Amazon's 2025-10 layoff of 14,000 corporate staff (Jassy's 2025-06 AI memo followed by 2025-11 "culture not AI" reframe, per Fortune 2025-11-01 and CNBC 2025-06-17) is referenced in §5 as a substance-vs-PR signal but not as a firm slot, because Amazon's AI-era collaboration model is not structurally distinct from Shopify's.

## 2. Firm-by-firm evidence

### 2.1 Shopify

**Team composition shifts.** 2023-05: ≈10% workforce cut (first post-pandemic restructuring) (Shopify Q1 2023 press release). 2024 through 2026-04: headcount roughly flat at 8,000-8,300 while GMV continued double-digit growth; internal Slack leaks and the published Lütke memo indicate engineering and support headcount tilted toward AI-leveraged generalists rather than specialized SDRs or tier-1 support.

**Reporting structure.** No public org-chart disclosure; inferred from internal memo culture that AI tooling adoption is treated as a line-manager-level objective rather than a separate AI function. No chief AI officer appointed as of 2026-04 (observed from public leadership pages).

**Hiring profile.** 2025-04-07 Lütke memo (published on X 2025-04-07 as `tobi/status/1909251946235437514`; reported by CNBC 2025-04-07 "Shopify CEO: Prove AI can't do jobs before asking for more headcount"): "Reflexive AI usage is now a baseline expectation at Shopify." Hiring requests must demonstrate the work cannot be done with AI before headcount is approved. Performance and peer review incorporate AI usage as a rated dimension.

**Promotion criteria.** Per the same memo, AI fluency is folded into the existing Shopify performance framework. No public detail on how AI-usage is scored; inferred to be manager discretion plus self-report.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** Not publicly attributed. The 2023-05 cut predates the AI-reflexive memo and was framed as focus / profitability. 2024-2026 attrition has been described as natural plus selective cuts; no explicit "AI replaced N people" statement.

**CEO positioning.** Lütke has been the cleanest public case of "AI-first hiring filter." The memo is a policy artifact, not a rhetorical flourish: it is cited by HR leaders and imitated by other CEOs through late 2025 (Fortune 2025-04-08 "Shopify CEO tells employees no new hires unless they can prove AI can't do the job"). By 2025-12, job postings industry-wide requiring AI skills had roughly doubled (from 5% to 9% per MIT CDO blog reference 2025-04-11 citing subsequent Lightcast data).

**Confidence.** H on the memo itself; M on whether reporting and promotion changed substantively (no leaked performance data).

### 2.2 Klarna

**Team composition shifts.** Headcount fell from 5,527 FTE (2022-12) to 3,422 FTE (2024-12), a ≈38% decline (Klarna 2024 annual report and IPO prospectus filed 2025-03; reported by CNBC 2025-05-14 "Klarna CEO says AI helped company shrink workforce by 40%"). Reduction combined a hiring freeze (started 2023-12 per Siemiatkowski) with 15-20% annual natural attrition. The oft-cited "AI does the work of 700 customer service agents" figure is from 2024-02-27 press release, based on one month of OpenAI-powered assistant handling ≈2.3 million chats.

**Reporting structure.** Customer experience reporting line was collapsed into the AI-product organization in 2024. By 2025-05 reversal, Klarna began piloting an "Uber-style" flexible remote-agent workforce reporting into a revived CX function (Bloomberg 2025-05 reported by Tech.co 2025-05 "Klarna Reverses AI Customer Service Replacement").

**Hiring profile.** Hiring resumed selectively in 2025-Q2 targeted at customer-support remote agents ("we want students, parents, rural workers" per Siemiatkowski interview with Bloomberg 2025-05-09 summarized in Fortune 2025-05-09 "Klarna plans to hire humans again").

**Promotion criteria.** No public disclosure.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** Highest-profile in the sample. The 2024-02 press release explicitly credited the AI assistant with "the equivalent of 700 agents." By 2025-05, Siemiatkowski publicly admitted "cost seems to have been a too predominant evaluation factor … what you end up having is lower quality" (Bloomberg interview, 2025-05-09, reported across outlets).

**CEO positioning.** Siemiatkowski is the archetypal case of aggressive public AI-replacement rhetoric followed by partial reversal. 2024-02: "AI did the job of 700 agents." 2025-02-03 Fortune: still warning AI will replace white-collar workers. 2025-05-09: "we went too far" and bringing humans back. 2026-02-17 Fortune: agreeing with Dario Amodei that white-collar workforce will shrink by a third by 2030, while reliant on natural attrition internally. The rhetoric swings but the headcount trend (downward) is consistent; the specific intervention that reversed is *quality of customer support*, not total headcount.

**Confidence.** H on the headcount trajectory and the 2025-05 reversal in customer support; M on whether the "Uber-style" pilot scaled or was itself quietly wound down.

### 2.3 Stripe

**Team composition shifts.** Headcount ≈8,570 at start of 2025 (efinancialcareers 2025-06 reporting Collison commentary). Engineers ≈40% of total (inferred ≈3,428). 2024: 1,145 pull requests per day average, disclosed by Patrick Collison publicly (efinancialcareers 2025-06) — this is a telemetric claim, not a staffing claim, but it anchors the productivity narrative.

**Reporting structure.** Applied ML team led by Sachin Bansal (formerly Apple and Google) became a central, cross-cutting function in 2024-2025. No public re-org announcement; inferred from job postings and executive quotes.

**Hiring profile.** Heavy PhD new-grad ML hiring at ≈$270k base (Stripe careers pages, 2025 listings). 2025-10-09 Fortune: Stripe's head of data and AI said she was hiring new grads "more than ever" and expressed anxiety about the Gen Z talent pipeline under AI disruption. 2026-01: Patrick Collison signalled 2026 would be Stripe's biggest hiring year, explicitly linking the expansion to AI-driven opportunity (blockchain.news flash news summary, 2026-01).

**Promotion criteria.** No public change disclosed.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** None. Stripe did reduce ≈14% of headcount in 2022-11 (pre-AI wave, framed as cost discipline) but has not announced AI-attributed cuts.

**CEO positioning.** Collison's position is the cleanest counter-example to the Lütke / Siemiatkowski rhetoric: "AI is driving us to hire more, not fewer." His much-memed "we need 500KG of engineers" line is internal-culture colour; the substantive claim is that productivity gains are reinvested into expansion, not headcount reduction.

**Confidence.** H on hiring expansion; M on whether this is truly different from Shopify in substance (both are expanding AI-skilled technical hiring; the difference is rhetorical framing — Shopify gates on AI-sufficiency, Stripe gates on AI-leveraged throughput).

### 2.4 Palantir

**Team composition shifts.** Palantir pioneered the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) / "Delta" role pre-2016 (Pragmatic Engineer newsletter on FDEs; Palantir careers). In 2024-2025, the FDE role became the growth hire as Palantir's commercial AIP (AI Platform) revenue compounded. 2024 and 2025 Palantir earnings calls emphasize FDE-led deployments as the unit of customer expansion.

**Reporting structure.** FDEs continue to report through the commercial (now Gotham and Foundry merged) go-to-market organization rather than through a central engineering function. The model is unchanged structurally since the 2010s; the AI wave raised its strategic visibility rather than modifying the structure.

**Hiring profile.** FDE listings emphasize "1+ year post-college" programming experience, strong LLM foundations, and customer-facing comfort (Palantir careers lever.co listings 2024-2025). Low tenure bar paired with high autonomy.

**Promotion criteria.** Promotion tied to account expansion and deployment outcomes, per public descriptions; no formal change.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** None observed; Palantir has grown headcount.

**CEO positioning.** Karp has positioned Palantir as *benefiting* from AI without structurally changing its operating model — "the FDE model was always AI-ready." This is the single strongest "structural continuity through AI transition" case in the sample.

**Confidence.** H.

### 2.5 Anthropic

**Team composition shifts.** 2023-01: ≈150 FTE (inferred from funding documents). 2025-Q4: ≈1,200-1,500 FTE range (inferred from reporting including Anthropic press and The Information 2025 coverage). Fastest-growing sub-organization in 2024-2025 has been Applied AI / Forward Deployed Engineering, which Anthropic publicly announced intent to "grow fivefold" in 2025 (reported by eweek 2025 "Why Forward-Deployed Engineers Are in High Demand"; Anthropic greenhouse job postings 2025).

**Reporting structure.** FDE organization spun up as a distinct function reporting into commercial / applied AI leadership (as opposed to reporting into research). Research, interpretability, and alignment continue to report separately. Notable that the 2024 arrival of Jan Leike from OpenAI created a scalable-oversight / automated-alignment unit on the research side.

**Hiring profile.** FDE: 3+ years technical customer-facing, strong prompting and agent dev, Python (Anthropic job board 2025). Research: traditional ML PhD pipeline plus poached senior researchers from OpenAI (Schulman 2024-08, Leike 2024-05, and others).

**Promotion criteria.** No public disclosure; inferred (from blog posts and public Sharma and other 2026 departure letters) that safety-oriented researchers feel the promotion and resource attention has tilted toward capabilities and applied. 2026-02-11 CNN coverage ("AI researchers are sounding the alarm on their way out the door"); 2026-02-12 Tech Brew coverage of Sharma resignation.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** None.

**CEO positioning.** Dario Amodei's 2025-05 Axios interview predicting 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs disappear has been a persistent public claim; internally Anthropic has not laid off, and continues to hire aggressively. The rhetoric is external strategic positioning (shaping policy and investor narrative) rather than internal HR action — a cleaner example of rhetoric-reality separation than Klarna, because Amodei is making a prediction about the economy, not about Anthropic.

**Confidence.** H on FDE growth and research departures; M on numerical headcount (Anthropic does not publish).

### 2.6 OpenAI

**Team composition shifts.** Headcount crossed ≈3,500 in 2025, per press reporting. Most consequential shifts are *departures*, not layoffs: Sutskever left 2024-05 to found Safe Superintelligence (raised $1B at $5B, then 2025 reports of $30B+); Jan Leike left 2024-05 to Anthropic; John Schulman left 2024-08 to Anthropic; Mira Murati (CTO), Bob McGrew (Chief Research Officer), Barret Zoph (VP Research) all departed in 2024. 2025: chief people officer Julia Villagra (2025-08), chief communications officer Hannah Wong, plus ≥7 researchers to Meta's Superintelligence Lab over mid-2025 (Fortune / Bloomberg 2025 reporting, summarized in digidai.github.io "OpenAI 2024-2025" 2026-03).

**Reporting structure.** Superalignment team disbanded 2024-05; successor Mission Alignment team disbanded 2026-02 (per Wired / TechCrunch 2026-02). Product organization substantially expanded under Kevin Weil and then subsequent product leaders.

**Hiring profile.** Heavy applied engineering and forward-deployed hiring through 2024-2025 paralleling Anthropic. Aggressive lateral hires from competing labs.

**Promotion criteria.** No public disclosure.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** None.

**CEO positioning.** Altman has consistently said AI will transform work but has avoided the specific "X people replaced" framing. On the AI-replacement rhetoric scale, Altman sits between Lütke (policy-oriented) and Amodei (macro-prediction).

**Confidence.** H on departures; M on remaining structural changes.

### 2.7 Cognition (Devin)

**Team composition shifts.** Small team; founded 2024-01, launched Devin demo 2024-03-12. Raised ≈$21M Founders Fund 2024-04; ≈$175M at $2B post-money 2024-04-late; reported ≈$400M at $10.2B post-money 2025-09 (Contrary Research and press). Acquired Windsurf 2025-07 (tens of millions ARR; hundreds of enterprise customers).

**Reporting structure.** Founder-led small organization; Windsurf team integrated as a product / IDE unit post-acquisition. No traditional middle-management layer through 2025-Q3 per reporting.

**Hiring profile.** Heavy on IOI medallists and competitive-programming pedigree (per founder statements and press profiles).

**Promotion criteria.** Not publicly disclosed.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** None.

**CEO positioning.** Scott Wu and team positioned Devin as a member-of-the-team abstraction ("the first AI software engineer"). By 2025-12 the messaging shifted toward "senior engineer + Devin" rather than "Devin autonomously" after independent testing revealed low completion rates.

**Substance-vs-PR note.** Answer.AI published testing showing Devin completed 3 of 20 assigned tasks (15%) in the months after launch (reported by Futurism, TweakTown late 2024). Cognition's own 2025 retrospective ("Devin's 2025 Performance Review") acknowledges that it excels on 4-8 hour well-scoped tasks but struggles with ambiguity and recursive complexity. ARR still grew from ≈$1M (2024-09) to ≈$73M (2025-06) to ≈$150M combined with Windsurf (2025-11 analyst estimates). The product works commercially despite the reliability critique — a case where capability shortfall was absorbed by narrowing the deployment envelope.

**Confidence.** H on ARR and reliability; M on team structure specifics.

### 2.8 Harvey + A&O Shearman

**Team composition shifts (at A&O Shearman).** 2023-02-15: Allen & Overy announced firmwide rollout of Harvey (LawSites reporting 2023-02). 2024: post Allen & Overy / Shearman & Sterling merger, enterprise deployment scaled to 4,000 staff across 43 jurisdictions; 2,000 lawyers using ContractMatrix daily (A&O Shearman AI page 2024-2025). Associate headcount at A&O Shearman was *not* cut attributable to Harvey through 2025-Q4 per publicly available data; instead, work absorbed at the lower end shifted toward higher-value review.

**Reporting structure (at Harvey).** Harvey built a "Domain Research" function hiring lawyers as product engineers — roughly 40-70 lawyer-engineers by mid-2025 per press reporting. This is a structurally new role: the bilingual lawyer-prompt-engineer. Reporting is into Harvey product leadership.

**Hiring profile.** A&O Shearman continues to hire traditional associates; Harvey hires lawyer-engineers and traditional software engineers. The observable shift is in the Harvey-side workforce, not the law-firm side.

**Promotion criteria.** No publicized change to A&O Shearman associate-to-partner track attributable to Harvey.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** None at A&O Shearman. Other law firms (BigLaw broadly) had selective 2024-2025 cuts of paralegals and document-review contractors, not associates.

**CEO positioning.** A&O Shearman leadership framed Harvey as a productivity multiplier for lawyers, never as a replacement. The fact-on-the-ground confirms this: associate recruiting targets for 2024 and 2025 classes were maintained.

**Confidence.** H on adoption figures reported by A&O Shearman; M on whether associate hiring targets shift in 2026-2027.

### 2.9 Sierra

**Team composition shifts.** Founded 2024-02 by Bret Taylor (ex-Salesforce co-CEO, ex-OpenAI board chair) and Clay Bavor (ex-Google). Hit $100M ARR in 7 quarters from launch (TechCrunch 2025-11-21); $350M raise at $10B valuation in 2025-09 (CNBC 2025-09-04). Team ≈30 in 2025-Q3, reported at >150 by early 2026 (job-listing aggregators).

**Reporting structure.** Engineering-led product org; customer-experience deployment via "Workspaces" model that introduces software-development-style collaboration between CX, ops, product, engineering.

**Hiring profile.** Senior engineers with conversational AI backgrounds; customer-facing agent-design roles.

**Promotion criteria.** Not publicly disclosed.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** None (too early).

**CEO positioning.** Taylor's positioning is outcome-based pricing + "agent is a product" framing (Cheeky Pint podcast 2025). He explicitly rejects the "replace headcount" framing, instead positioning Sierra as CX *capacity* expansion for clients. This sits in contrast to Klarna's 2024 framing.

**Confidence.** H on growth; M on team structure (not disclosed in detail).

### 2.11 Replit

**Team composition shifts.** 2024-Q2 layoff: ≈130 → ≈65 FTE, ≈50% reduction (Semafor 2025-01-15 "Replit CEO on AI breakthroughs"; GreyJournal "From Layoffs to Liftoff"). The remaining team was organized as an Agent Task Force around the new coding-agent product. HQ moved out of San Francisco in 2024-04. ARR grew from $2.8M (2023) to $150M annualized (2024-Q4 to 2025-Q1) after the Agent launch 2024-09.

**Reporting structure.** Agent Task Force became the de facto primary engineering axis; non-Agent staff reported to feel "demoralized" per Semafor reporting, which correlated with the departures during the 2024 layoff.

**Hiring profile.** Post-layoff team reported to be heavily senior; explicit statements from Masad that professional coders are not the core user ("We don't care about professional coders anymore," Semafor 2025-01-15) sharpened hiring toward product-builders rather than traditional dev-tools engineers.

**Promotion criteria.** Not publicly disclosed.

**Layoffs attributed to AI.** Yes — the 2024-Q2 cut was framed internally as restructuring around the agent opportunity, which is an AI-induced restructuring even though the public framing emphasized focus rather than AI-replaces-human.

**CEO positioning.** Masad's Semafor statements 2025-01 and 2025-05 are some of the sharpest "software developers will not be needed" quotes in the sample. Substantively, Replit's internal team is *majority software developers building the agent*.

**Confidence.** H.

**Promotion criteria.** No public change to up-or-out; however, AI-proficiency is increasingly a partner-track evaluation factor. Not yet a formal criterion.

Anecdotal reporting (The Information, 2025) suggests some reduction in first-year analyst hiring at BigLaw and selected cuts at traditional consulting but not in the MBB tier.

**Confidence.** H on platform adoption; L on structural ratio change in pyramid hiring.

## 3. Collaboration model taxonomy

Five distinct patterns emerge from the evidence. The taxonomy names each pattern, identifies the firms that exemplify it, and notes the structural signature.

### Pattern 1 — Embedded forward-deployed (FDE) model

**Structural signature.** Senior technical staff embed physically or operationally with customer teams, shipping production code inside the customer's environment. Reports into commercial leadership, not into the central engineering function of the vendor.

**Firms.** Palantir (originator, 2010s-present), Anthropic (2024-2026 "grow fivefold" FDE build), OpenAI (parallel FDE scale), Cohere (referenced in eweek reporting).

**Observed substrate.** FDE job listings grew ≈800% (January through September 2025) across the segment (Fonzi AI / Pragmatic Engineer analysis 2025). Palantir's unchanged structure is the control condition — the model survived the AI capability wave without redesign.

**Confidence.** H.

### Pattern 2 — AI-first hiring filter (productivity-gate model)

**Structural signature.** Organization continues to grow but imposes an AI-sufficiency test before any headcount addition. AI usage is a promotion-evaluation input. Layoffs are not the mechanism; attrition-through-non-replacement plus selective over-hiring in AI-leveraged roles does the work.

**Firms.** Shopify (cleanest case, 2025-04 memo), Stripe (inverse-rhetoric variant — "hire more because AI opens opportunity"), Amazon (2025 variant — Jassy's 2025-06 memo and subsequent middle-manager cuts, though publicly reframed 2025-11 as "culture").

**Observed substrate.** The trend is primarily a hiring policy, not a structural redesign. Performance reviews have shifted slower than hiring memos.

**Confidence.** H on Shopify; M on whether the filter holds through 2026 capability shifts.

### Pattern 3 — Agent product + thin human wrapper

**Structural signature.** The operating company narrows its human headcount to the portion that (a) builds the agent, (b) supervises and escalates edge cases, and (c) sells. First-line operations are agent-executed. Small human operator teams in escalation-response.

**Firms.** Klarna (customer service, partially reversed), Sierra (customer experience agents for enterprise clients), Replit (Agent-Task-Force reorganization), Cursor (agentic coding assistance, narrow human core).

**Observed substrate.** The pattern compounds through product revenue growth (Sierra $100M ARR in 7Q, Replit $2.8M → $150M, Cursor $1B → $2B ARR). Klarna's reversal is notable because the customer-experience variant proved brittle where the developer-productivity and agent-platform variants did not.

**Confidence.** H on growth metrics; M on whether Sierra-style enterprise-CX deployment reproduces Klarna's quality regression under scale.

### Pattern 4 — Concentrated senior + acquisition-based expansion

**Structural signature.** Founder-led, flat, senior-heavy team that expands by acquiring complementary teams rather than by traditional hiring. Middle-management layer either skipped or minimized.

**Firms.** Cursor / Anysphere (Supermaven, Koala, Graphite acquisitions 2024-2025), Cognition (Windsurf acquisition 2025-07), Sierra (smaller but similar pattern with selective poaching).

**Observed substrate.** The acquisition cadence is ≥ 1 per year once the firm crosses ≈$50M ARR. Acquired talent is integrated into a senior-heavy band rather than forming new middle layers.

**Confidence.** H on acquisition pattern; M on whether this model scales past ≈200 FTE.

### Pattern 5 — Firmwide AI platform + preserved pyramid

**Structural signature.** Deploy an internal AI platform (Lilli, GENE, Zora, ChatGPT Enterprise) firmwide, report adoption metrics quarterly, preserve traditional headcount pyramid, rebrand tech-share of revenue upward without restructuring the career ladder.

**Firms.** McKinsey QuantumBlack (Lilli), BCG X (GENE), Bain (ChatGPT Enterprise + OpenAI alliance), Deloitte (Zora AI). Adjacent: A&O Shearman (Harvey).

**Observed substrate.** Platform usage metrics are high and verifiable. Career-ladder restructuring has been talked about but not observed through 2025-Q4. The first-year analyst / associate hiring at Tier-2 professional services is showing mild softening per industry-recruiting data, but not at MBB or magic-circle tier.

**Confidence.** H on platform adoption; L on pyramid change.

## 4. Resilient vs fragile models

**Criteria for resilience.** A collaboration model is "resilient" if it survived at least two capability shifts (e.g., GPT-4 → GPT-4o → GPT-5, or pre-agents → post-agents) without a public redesign. "Fragile" if it required visible redesign within 12 months.

### 4.1 Resilient models

**R1. Embedded FDE model (Pattern 1).** The Palantir / Anthropic / OpenAI FDE structure has absorbed GPT-4, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Claude 4, and post-reasoning-model capabilities without restructuring. The FDE acts as a shock absorber between capability shift and customer-facing change — the reporting line is stable, the job description evolves at the content level rather than the structure level. **Confidence H.**

**R2. Firmwide AI platform + preserved pyramid (Pattern 5).** McKinsey has upgraded Lilli through multiple model generations (GPT-4 → GPT-4o → GPT-5 variants, plus Claude and internal fine-tunes) without altering consultant career ladder. BCG and Bain similarly. The platform absorbs model churn; the organization does not have to. **Confidence H on resilience so far; M on whether this holds through an agentic shift that replaces analyst-level synthesis entirely.**

**R3. Concentrated senior + acquisition-based expansion (Pattern 4).** Cursor and Cognition have absorbed capability shifts (Claude 3.5 → Claude 4 → reasoning models) by acquiring complementary teams and adjusting product, not by redesigning internal org. The small size of the resident core is itself the adaptability feature. **Confidence M; the model has not yet been tested at 500+ FTE.**

### 4.2 Fragile models

**F1. Agent product + thin human wrapper in customer-service domain (Pattern 3, CX variant).** Klarna is the documented failure case. The thin-wrapper model assumed agent quality that the 2024 model generation could sustain on average but not on tail cases. Empathy and nuance failures drove measurable customer-satisfaction decline; the reversal began 2025-05 with the rehiring pilot. **Confidence H.**

**F2. AI-first hiring filter when paired with public "AI replaces" rhetoric (Pattern 2, rhetoric variant).** Shopify's memo has held because the memo is a *filter*, not a replacement claim. Siemiatkowski's public posture (replacement-then-reversal) is less durable — rhetoric creates pressure to deliver visible replacement outcomes, which then rebounds when quality slips. **Confidence M.**

**F3. Agent-task-force reorganization that marginalizes non-agent staff (Pattern 3, internal variant).** Replit halved its team in 2024-Q2. The 2025 revenue growth validated the commercial decision, but the reporting about team demoralization suggests the model cost institutional memory that may matter in a future downturn. **Confidence L on long-term fragility; H on the short-term redesign cost.**

### 4.3 Unclear

**U1. Sierra's enterprise-CX thin-wrapper variant.** Bret Taylor's 2025-09 $10B valuation round requires the model to scale into new verticals without reproducing the Klarna quality regression. Unclear-to-resilient. **Confidence L.**

**U2. Anthropic / OpenAI dual FDE + research organization under continuing safety-staff attrition.** The FDE side is resilient; the research side has had persistent senior departures. The collaboration model is dual, and whether it remains viable depends on whether the research side can rebuild coherence. **Confidence L.**

## 5. Substance vs PR filter

**Rule of thumb.** Classify a firm as "substance" if the org-chart and hiring data match the public claim within ±20%; "PR" if the rhetoric outruns observable restructuring by more than that; "mixed" if one dimension is real and another is rhetorical.

### Substance (observable restructuring matches rhetoric)

- **Shopify.** Memo is a filter; hiring policy changed; AI usage is in performance review. On-the-ground evidence matches.
- **Palantir.** FDE model is not a PR claim; it is a pre-existing structure reaffirmed.
- **Harvey / A&O Shearman.** Adoption numbers (4,000 staff, 2,000 daily ContractMatrix users, 30% contract review reduction) are verifiable via A&O Shearman disclosures.
- **Replit.** The layoff happened and the agent product carried the revenue.
- **McKinsey Lilli / BCG GENE / Bain ChatGPT / Deloitte Zora.** Adoption rollouts are verifiable; where the rhetoric is modest ("AI as multiplier") the substance matches.

### PR (rhetoric outruns observed restructuring)

- **Klarna (2024 rhetoric phase).** The "700 agents replaced" framing implied a stable steady-state; the 2025-05 reversal shows the steady state did not hold. The headcount *trajectory* is real (40% down) but the specific 700-agents claim was operationally brittle. Mixed-to-PR.
- **Amazon (2025-06 Jassy memo vs 2025-11 "culture" reframe).** The original memo explicitly attributed future workforce reduction to AI efficiency. The 14,000-role cut in October 2025 was subsequently reframed by Jassy as culture-driven (Fortune 2025-11-01, citing Q3 earnings call). The substance (cuts happened) is real; the causal attribution has been rhetorically repainted. Mixed with a PR lean.
- **Perplexity / Aravind Srinivas.** Public statements about "most people don't enjoy their jobs" and "entry-level jobs disappearing" are macro-rhetoric untethered to Perplexity internal restructuring (Perplexity continues to expand). This is *policy-adjacent rhetoric*, not PR about the firm itself — but it should be read with the gap in mind.
- **Cognition / Devin marketing.** The "first AI software engineer" frame outran the 15% task-completion rate demonstrated in independent testing. Cognition's own 2025 retrospective repositions Devin toward well-scoped tasks. Mixed: the product exists and grew commercially, but the founding marketing outran reliability.

### Mixed signals

- **Anthropic.** FDE growth is substance. Amodei's "50% of entry-level jobs gone" is rhetoric about the economy, not Anthropic itself. The distinction matters: Anthropic internally does not model the replacement pattern it predicts externally.
- **Stripe.** Collison's hiring-expansion framing is substance (PR count up, hiring up, ML team up). But it does not tell us what the Stripe engineer workforce is internally *doing differently* — the productivity telemetry is evidence of throughput, not of structural collaboration change.

### Consulting caveat

The consulting triad (McKinsey, Bain, BCG) falls into substance on platform adoption and into *not-yet-tested* on pyramid restructuring. The absence of layoff rhetoric and the absence of pyramid restructuring are consistent. The possibility that the 2026-2027 analyst class will be quietly smaller — without an announcement — is live, and is worth monitoring as a PR-lagging-substance case.

## 6. Open questions and negative evidence

**Q1. Does the AI-first hiring filter (Shopify) produce a measurable productivity gain distinct from the underlying AI-tooling effect?** No firm has published a clean before/after productivity metric isolating the filter from the tools. **Negative evidence.**

**Q2. At what scale does the concentrated-senior + acquisition-based model (Cursor, Cognition) break?** The model has not been observed at >300 FTE. Cognition reaching ≈$150M ARR with ≈100 FTE is extreme by traditional SaaS ratios; it remains unclear whether the ratio survives enterprise-contract complexity. **Negative evidence — no disconfirming case yet.**

**Q3. What happens to A&O Shearman's associate class of 2026 and 2027?** Recruitment targets for 2024 and 2025 were maintained despite the Harvey rollout. If 2026 and 2027 targets are reduced, the "Pattern 5: preserved pyramid" model fails its first real stress test. **Monitoring required.**

**Q4. Does the Sierra enterprise CX thin-wrapper model reproduce Klarna's quality regression at scale?** Sierra's $100M ARR in 7 quarters is impressive but the 2026 customer-satisfaction data is not yet available. **Open.**

**Q5. Did OpenAI's research-side attrition inflict measurable capability loss, or was the capability carried by scale?** Unknowable without internal benchmark data. The fact that GPT-5 shipped on schedule (late 2025) and remained competitive is evidence the organization absorbed the departures, but it does not close the question. **Open.**

**Q6. Specific absent data.** Pyramid composition at MBB consulting 2024-2026; Anthropic / OpenAI / Stripe headcount by department; Klarna's customer-satisfaction time series 2023-2026; Cursor / Anysphere exact FTE count; Harvey-side lawyer-engineer hiring rate with confirmed numbers. **All sought, not found in public disclosure.**

**Q7. Negative firms.** Meta's 2025 "Superintelligence Labs" hiring spree, xAI's rapid expansion, and Google DeepMind's internal reorganization were reviewed and excluded — they are frontier-lab variants of patterns already captured. Apple is deliberately not in scope because its AI collaboration model is not publicly legible as of 2026-04. **Documented exclusions.**

## 7. Feedback to three-pillar adaptability frame

1. **Pillar 2 (Organization) and Pillar 1 (AI usage) couple through the hiring filter.** Shopify's AI-first filter is simultaneously an organizational policy and an AI-usage policy. The frame's clean pillar separation is analytically useful but, in practice, firms operate the two pillars jointly through hiring and promotion mechanisms. M1's causal-loop diagram should include a named feedback loop: `AI usage maturity → hiring filter tightness → AI-leveraged hires → AI usage maturity`.

2. **The FDE pattern is the strongest "structure outlives capability shift" evidence in the corpus.** It merits explicit treatment as a Pillar 2 invariant across capability transitions. The O2 role-redefinition memo should cross-reference §3.1 of this memo and treat FDE as a meta-role whose substantive content changes while the shell persists.

3. **The Klarna reversal is a Pillar-2 failure traceable to a Pillar-1 mis-calibration.** The firm had AI capability sufficient for a median customer interaction but not for the tail. The organizational redesign got ahead of the AI-usage safety envelope. M2's adaptation-to-overcorrection ratio should include Klarna as a named case.

4. **Consulting Pattern 5 (firmwide platform + preserved pyramid) is the cleanest separation of tooling change from organizational change.** It supports the claim that *tooling adaptability can substitute for organizational adaptability in the short run, at the cost of accumulated organizational technical debt*. This sharpens the adaptability frame: tooling change is cheaper than org change, so firms that can route adaptability through tooling will prefer to; the question is how long they can before structural change is forced. M1 should include a "tooling-absorbs-adaptation" balancing loop with an associated delay.

5. **Public CEO rhetoric systematically diverges from internal restructuring.** The substance-vs-PR filter in §5 should be promoted to a first-class evidence-hygiene rule for the entire adaptability track. Proposed addition to `research/operations/expansion/evidence-tier-method.md`: when a claim is grounded in CEO public positioning only, downgrade its evidence tier unless corroborated by org-chart or hiring data.

6. **Scale-tier effects are visible in the taxonomy.** Pattern 4 (concentrated senior + acquisitions) is S1-S2 only; Pattern 1 (FDE) spans S2-S6; Pattern 3 (agent product + thin wrapper) spans S1-S4; Pattern 5 (firmwide platform + pyramid) is S4-S7. The scale-based track's prediction that resilience scales with coordination capital (S7 international) and speed capital (S1 solo) is partially borne out — with a notable middle-tier (S3-S4) fragility where Klarna's customer-service thin-wrapper failed.
