# A3 — Workflow Retooling Across Knowledge-Work Domains

axis: A (Pillar 1 — AI Usage)
date: 2026-04-22
plan: research/operations/expansion/adaptability-framework-research-plan.md

## 1. Method

This memo asks how five knowledge-work domains — legal, medical, finance, software engineering, and consulting — rebuilt their daily workflows between 2023 and 2026 as foundation-model capabilities moved from GPT-3.5-class chat assistants to long-context, tool-using, and agentic systems. The research object is *workflow architecture* — the sequence of human steps, AI steps, review gates, and artifacts produced — and the *organizational mechanics* that produced the change — champions, centers of excellence (CoE), vendor relationships, migration style, retooling velocity, and rework cost.

Evidence was collected via targeted web search on 2026-04-22 against a seed list from the parent research plan (Harvey/A&O Shearman, Thomson Reuters CoCounsel, Ironclad, Robin AI; Abridge, Nabla, Microsoft DAX/Dragon Copilot, Epic; BloombergGPT, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan LLM Suite, Goldman Sachs; GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Cognition Devin; McKinsey Lilli/QuantumBlack, BCG Deckster/GENE, Deloitte Zora). Every factual claim below carries an inline URL citation to the source used. Claims are tagged **observed** (reported in a cited source) or **inferred** (my synthesis from observed material) and are labeled **high / medium / low** confidence. Dates are absolute; where a source says "last month" I have converted to calendar dates from the surrounding text. Primary evidence window is 2022–2026, matching the research plan.

Three caveats apply throughout:

- Vendor-published metrics (hours saved, adoption percentages, productivity lift) are self-reported and were treated as **medium-confidence** unless a third-party or peer-reviewed source confirmed them. Where an independent randomized study exists (METR for coding agents, the UCLA DAX-vs-Nabla RCT for ambient scribes, the Mass General Brigham / Permanente burnout studies, the BCG–Harvard-Wharton study for consulting), the randomized number is the anchor and vendor numbers are reported as context.
- The domain sample is skewed toward US and UK anchor firms with English-language public disclosures. A3 signals from Japanese, Korean, and continental European adopters are weaker because fewer firms publish adoption data at the level of migration style or rework cost.
- "Retooling velocity" is measured as *time from capability release to production-scale workflow change* where that date is publicly reported; otherwise I report the first dated production deploy and label velocity qualitatively.

## 2. Domain-by-domain evidence

### 2.1 Legal — contract review and due diligence

**Pre-AI workflow snapshot (2022).** Contract review and due diligence were organized around tiered associate review: junior associates read first pass, senior associates second pass, partner sign-off. A mid-size M&A diligence job might commit 6–20 associates for 1–4 weeks, with review checklists and precedent libraries maintained inside document management systems such as iManage and practice-group wikis. Billing-hour incentives made the workflow economically resistant to compression. (**inferred from practice, high confidence**)

**Retooling events 2023–2026.**

- *February 2023* — Allen & Overy (now A&O Shearman) announces the first magic-circle deployment of Harvey, after a beta starting November 2022; by announcement roughly 3,500 lawyers had run ~40,000 queries ([aoshearman.com](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/news/ao-announces-exclusive-launch-partnership-with-harvey)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *November 2023* — A&O Shearman launches **ContractMatrix** with Harvey and Microsoft, embedding Harvey's API into a grounded contract-review product ([harvey.ai](https://www.harvey.ai/customers/a-and-o-shearman)). By 2024, A&O reports 4,000+ lawyers across 43 jurisdictions saving 2–3 hours per week, a 30% reduction in contract-review time, and 7-hour average savings on complex document analysis ([medium.com/takafumi.endo](https://medium.com/@takafumi.endo/how-harvey-built-trust-in-legal-ai-a-case-study-for-builders-786cc23c3b6d)). **Observed, medium confidence** — vendor-reported.
- *October 2023* — PwC announces a strategic alliance with OpenAI and Harvey across its Global Tax, Legal, and HR practices, building on a March 2023 Harvey rollout to the 4,000-lawyer tax-legal group ([pwc.com](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2023/pwc-announces-strategic-alliance-with-harvey-positioning-pwcs-legal-business-solutions-at-the-forefront-of-legal-generative-ai.html)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *2023–2025* — Thomson Reuters CoCounsel, launched 2023, reaches 1,000,000 professional users across 107 countries by February 2026 ([prnewswire.com](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/one-million-professionals-turn-to-cocounsel-as-thomson-reuters-scales-ai-for-regulated-industries-302694903.html)); ~36% of law firms use the Core CoCounsel tool, though only ~25% of those firms have fully deployed it to attorneys or staff as of September 2025 ([bestlawfirms.com](https://www.bestlawfirms.com/articles/the-ai-adoption-curve-in-law/6934)). **Observed, high confidence** on total, **medium** on depth-of-deployment.
- *April 2025* — A&O Shearman and Harvey announce a profit-sharing partnership to co-develop agentic multi-step workflow tools, initially in antitrust filings, cybersecurity, fund formation, and loan review ([legaltechnology.com](https://legaltechnology.com/2025/04/07/ao-shearman-partners-with-harvey-to-launch-practice-based-workflow-tools/), [artificiallawyer.com](https://www.artificiallawyer.com/2025/04/07/ao-shearman-to-profit-share-with-harvey-on-agentic-tools/)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *2024–2025* — Ironclad adds **Jurist** (2024) and AI Playbooks for auto-extraction of 190+ metadata elements and clause redlining, named Leader in 2025 Gartner Magic Quadrant for CLM and Forrester Wave Q1 2025 ([ironcladapp.com](https://ironcladapp.com/resources/articles/best-legal-ai-software)). Robin AI pairs contract review with human-in-the-loop managed services for financial-services clients ([legalontech.com](https://www.legalontech.com/post/best-ai-contract-review-tools)). Independent trend reporting puts 2024–2025 AI-assisted contract-review cycle-time reduction at 75–85% for standardized contracts ([legalontech.com](https://www.legalontech.com/post/best-ai-contract-review-tools)). **Observed for tool features, inferred for time-reduction magnitude, medium confidence.**
- *2023–2025* — Rising rate of sanctions for AI-hallucinated citations: Damien Charlotin's AI-Hallucination-Cases database tracks 850+ documented court filings containing hallucinated AI citations, with reported incidence rising from roughly two per week before spring 2025 to two to three per day later in 2025; sanctions include $10,000 (*Noland v. Land of the Free*) and $31,100 (California special-master order) ([damiencharlotin.com](https://www.damiencharlotin.com/hallucinations/), [lawnext.com 2025-09](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/09/a-new-wrinkle-in-ai-hallucination-cases-lawyers-dinged-for-failing-to-detect-opponents-fake-citations.html), [lawfuel.com](https://www.lawfuel.com/lawyers-sanctioned-again-for-relying-on-bogus-legal-ai-citations/)). A separate analysis claims Harvey hallucinates in roughly one of every six legal queries, though Harvey disputes it; I report this as **observed-contested, low confidence** ([tao-hpu.medium.com](https://tao-hpu.medium.com/harvey-ai-hit-8-billion-its-tools-still-hallucinate-in-one-of-every-six-queries-812d64182dc4)).

**Example firms.** A&O Shearman, PwC Legal, Kirkland & Ellis (BTI "Best of the Best" for Generative AI 2024, per firm announcement) ([kirkland.com](https://www.kirkland.com/news/award/2024/03/leading-ai-and-legal-tech-advisors-2024)), DLA Piper (proprietary tool track), BLG (CoCounsel firmwide, December 2025) ([blg.com](https://www.blg.com/en/about-us/news/2025/12/blg-expands-long-standing-thomson-reuters-partnership-with-firmwide-rollout-of-cocounsel)).

**Migration style.** **Continuous integration** dominates. Launches are staged: beta → named practice-group pilot (tax, antitrust, M&A) → firm-wide rollout of a grounded product (ContractMatrix, CoCounsel) → agentic feature addition on the same substrate. No magic-circle firm executed a "big-bang" replacement of manual review; even the A&O–Harvey agentic rollout in April 2025 was added as a practice-specific layer on top of existing ContractMatrix. (**observed / inferred, high confidence**)

**Champion / CoE / vendor role.** A dedicated innovation unit — A&O's Markets Innovation Group, PwC's Global Tax and Legal Services technology team, Thomson Reuters' professional-services division — played the champion role. The CoE pattern in legal is **vendor-led and firm-co-developed**: the firm anchors domain expertise, the vendor anchors model and product, profit-sharing arrangements (A&O–Harvey 2025) formalize the split. (**observed, high confidence**)

**Retooling velocity.** **Medium.** First production deploy lagged the GPT-4 release by roughly 3–6 months at the very front (A&O ContractMatrix, November 2023, vs. GPT-4 release March 2023), and rollout to 50% of large-firm lawyers took roughly 18–24 months (CoCounsel adoption curve). Agentic-workflow reframing lagged o1-class reasoning models by 4–7 months (A&O–Harvey profit-sharing launch April 2025 vs. o1 release late 2024). (**inferred from dates, medium confidence**)

**Rework cost.** **Medium to high** on the back end. The hallucination-sanction cases function as an accumulating rework tax: every fabricated citation sanctioned creates a new mandatory attorney-verification step, which partially undoes the hours saved by AI drafting. The profit-sharing agentic upgrade also implies the original Harvey chat-assistant layer is being partly redesigned rather than extended. (**inferred, medium confidence**)

### 2.2 Medical — clinical documentation and imaging

**Pre-AI workflow snapshot (2022).** Clinician documentation was dominated by after-hours EHR note-writing ("pajama time"), voice dictation via Dragon Medical One, and human medical scribes in high-volume specialties. Imaging workflows centered on PACS, radiology information systems, and dictated reports; AI-assisted image analysis existed for narrow indications (stroke triage, DR screening) but was not yet integrated into primary read workflows. (**inferred from practice, high confidence**)

**Retooling events 2023–2026.**

- *2023* — Nuance (Microsoft) begins ambient documentation pilots with DAX. **January 2024**: general availability of DAX Copilot embedded in Epic; Stanford Medicine and WellSpan Health announce enterprise-wide deployments ([prnewswire.com 2024-01](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nuance-announces-general-availability-of-dax-copilot-embedded-in-epic-transforming-healthcare-experiences-with-automated-clinical-documentation-302037590.html), [microsoft.com 2024-03](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2024/03/11/microsoft-makes-the-promise-of-ai-in-healthcare-real-through-new-collaborations-with-healthcare-organizations-and-partners/)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *February 2024* — Abridge announces "Abridge Inside" integration with Epic from Haiku to Hyperdrive and becomes Epic's first "Pal" in the Partners and Pals program ([businesswire.com](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240213503774/en/Abridge-Announces-Abridge-Inside-with-Epic-Integration-from-Haiku-to-Hyperdrive), [abridge.com](https://www.abridge.com/press-release/abridge-becomes-epics-first-pal-bringing-generative-ai-to-more-providers-and-patients)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *2024–2025* — Abridge deploys enterprise-wide at Duke Health, Johns Hopkins, Mayo Clinic, UNC Health, UI Health, and in October 2025 commits to rollout across Northwell Health's 28 hospitals ([abridge.com Northwell](https://www.abridge.com/press-release/northwell-scales-abridge), [hlth.com](https://hlth.com/insights/news/ui-health-to-deploy-abridge-ai-directly-into-epic-ehr-enterprise-wide-2025-11-25)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *2024* — Nabla Copilot reaches 20,000 providers within 10 months of launch ([venturebeat.com](https://venturebeat.com/ai/nabla-the-ai-copilot-for-doctors-nets-24m-to-transform-medical-consultations)); by June 2025 piloted in 80+ organizations including CHLA, Kaiser Permanente, Stratum Med, Carle Health; Series C $70M close ([statnews.com](https://www.statnews.com/2025/06/17/nabla-raises-70-million-ambient-market-heats-up/)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *March 2025* — Microsoft unifies Dragon Medical One and DAX into **Dragon Copilot**; over 3 million ambient patient conversations across 600 healthcare organizations reported in a single month by 2025 ([news.microsoft.com](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2025/03/03/microsoft-dragon-copilot-provides-the-healthcare-industrys-first-unified-voice-ai-assistant-that-enables-clinicians-to-streamline-clinical-documentation-surface-information-and-automate-task/), [microsoft.com/health-solutions](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/health-solutions/clinical-workflow/dragon-copilot)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *November 2024 – January 2025* — UCLA randomized clinical trial (238 physicians, 14 specialties, 23,653 visits) compares DAX vs. Nabla vs. usual care; Nabla associated with a **9.5% decrease in time-in-note** vs. control, DAX not significantly different from control, both associated with potential reductions in burnout and task load ([ai.nejm.org](https://ai.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/AIoa2501000), [llmsfordoctors.com](https://www.llmsfordoctors.com/trials/ambient-ai-scribes-dax-nabla-rct-2025/)). **Observed, high confidence** — peer-reviewed, randomized.
- *2024–2025* — Permanente Medical Group 63-week evaluation Oct 2023 – Dec 2024: 7,260 physicians, 2,576,627 encounters; statistically significant reductions in note-time, time per appointment, and pajama time; 84% positive effect on communication, 82% work-satisfaction improvement ([abridge.com](https://www.abridge.com/press-release/abridge-inside-for-inpatient-and-outpatient-orders)). Mass General Brigham and Emory studies report 21.2% and 30.7% absolute reductions in burnout / documentation-wellbeing at 84 days ([massgeneralbrigham.org](https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/ambient-documentation-technologies-reduce-physician-burnout), [emoryhealthcare.org](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/newsroom/2025/08/study-finds-ambient-documentation-technology-reduces-burnout-and-increases-well-being-in-clinicians)). Cross-system QI study of 263 clinicians: 30-day ambulatory burnout 51.9% → 38.8% ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov PMC12492056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12492056/)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *Imaging 2024–2025* — GE HealthCare retains top FDA-authorization share in radiology AI (115 authorizations including acquisitions of Bay Labs, BK Medical, Caption Health, MIM Software, icometrix, Spectronic Medical); total FDA AI-enabled device authorizations cross 1,000 in 2025 ([theimagingwire.com](https://theimagingwire.com/2025/12/10/ai-enabled-medical-devices-granted-fda-marketing-authorization/), [intuitionlabs.ai](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ai-radiology-trends-2025)). Signa One MRI with AI-assisted patient positioning and motion-sync unveiled at RSNA 2025 ([fiercebiotech.com](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/ge-healthcare-philips-siemens-spotlight-ai-forward-imaging-rsna-2025), [investor.gehealthcare.com](https://investor.gehealthcare.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ge-healthcare-builds-century-innovation-shape-future-radiology)). **Observed, high confidence.**

**Example firms.** Stanford Medicine, WellSpan (DAX 2024); Permanente, Duke, Johns Hopkins, Mayo, UNC, Northwell (Abridge); Kaiser Permanente, Carle Health, CHLA (Nabla); GE HealthCare, Philips, Siemens (imaging).

**Migration style.** **Continuous integration, EHR-substrate-locked.** Every large clinical-documentation deploy is gated through Epic's integration program (Haiku/Hyperdrive/Workshop/Partners-and-Pals), and rollouts are specialty-by-specialty (ambulatory → inpatient → ED → orders). Imaging AI is integrated as an additive overlay on PACS rather than a replacement. Big-bang migration does not occur because the EHR is the organization's system of record and cannot be hot-swapped. (**observed / inferred, high confidence**)

**Champion / CoE / vendor role.** The pattern is **vendor + EHR-partner + clinical-informatics CoE**. The CMIO / chief clinical informatics officer function, often in partnership with a health-system's "digital transformation office," is the organizational champion; Epic is the integration gatekeeper; Microsoft/Nuance, Abridge, and Nabla are the model/app vendors. Unlike legal, individual clinician champions matter less than system-level CMIO buy-in because rollout depends on EHR-template changes and credentialing. (**inferred, high confidence**)

**Retooling velocity.** **Slow to medium.** Lag from capability release to production is structurally long: DAX Copilot production GA followed GPT-4 release by roughly 10 months, and adoption across 600 organizations took another 12+ months. Imaging AI operates on FDA 510(k) timelines (months to years), so per-capability retooling is slower again. Within a health system, 30–84-day evaluation periods are the cadence, not per-model-release swaps. (**inferred from dates, high confidence**)

**Rework cost.** **Low for ambient scribes, medium for imaging.** Ambient documentation produces reversible, incremental change — a health system can pause rollout with minimal capital loss. Imaging AI carries regulatory-reacquisition cost when a model is materially updated (510(k) predicate analysis). Vendor-consolidation risk (Microsoft Dragon Copilot absorbing DAX; GE HealthCare absorbing six AI imaging vendors) implies concentrated rework risk on vendor strategy changes. (**inferred, medium confidence**)

### 2.3 Finance — equity research, compliance, underwriting, wealth management

**Pre-AI workflow snapshot (2022).** Sell-side equity research generated 100,000+ analyst reports per large bank per year; retrieval by advisors was low — Morgan Stanley later reported internal access rates of roughly 20% against the firm's research corpus ([openai.com/morgan-stanley](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/)). Investment banking junior analysts built pitch decks by manual comparable-company extraction from Capital IQ / FactSet. Compliance teams reviewed communications and trade surveillance manually or via rule-based NLP. (**inferred from practice, high confidence**)

**Retooling events 2023–2026.**

- *March 2023* — Bloomberg announces BloombergGPT, a 50-billion-parameter finance-trained LLM, ~$10M training cost ([cnbc.com 2023-04](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/13/bloomberg-plans-to-integrate-gpt-style-a-i-into-its-terminal.html), [medium.com/arjun_shah](https://medium.com/@arjun_shah/bloombergs-10m-data-experiment-8c552ca5c212)). In internal benchmarks, general-purpose GPT-4 without domain data outperforms BloombergGPT on most financial tasks including FinQA zero-shot ([medium.com/arjun_shah](https://medium.com/@arjun_shah/bloombergs-10m-data-experiment-8c552ca5c212)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *September 2023* — Morgan Stanley Wealth Management launches AI @ Morgan Stanley Assistant on GPT-4 to ~16,000 financial advisors; grounds on ~100,000 research reports ([cnbc.com 2023-09](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/09/18/morgan-stanley-chatgpt-financial-advisors.html)). Research-document access jumps from 20% to 80% per firm reporting ([openai.com/morgan-stanley](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/)). **Observed, high confidence** on dates and deployment; **medium confidence** on 20%→80% (vendor-reported).
- *March–June 2024* — Morgan Stanley launches AI @ Morgan Stanley **Debrief**, piloting March 2024, firmwide by June 2024; drafts meeting summaries, email follow-ups, Salesforce notes ([cnbc.com 2024-06](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/06/26/morgan-stanley-openai-powered-assistant-for-wealth-advisors.html), [morganstanley.com Debrief launch](https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/ai-at-morgan-stanley-debrief-launch)). Adoption reported at 98% of advisor teams ([reruption.com](https://reruption.com/en/knowledge/industry-cases/morgan-stanleys-ai-debrief-98-advisor-adoption-boost)). **Observed, medium-to-high confidence.**
- *October 2024* — Morgan Stanley extends AI tooling to Institutional Securities with **AskResearchGPT** ([cnbc.com 2024-10](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/10/23/morgan-stanley-rolls-out-openai-powered-chatbot-for-wall-street-division.html), [morganstanley.com AskResearchGPT](https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/morgan-stanley-research-announces-askresearchgpt)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *Summer 2024 onward* — JPMorgan Chase releases **LLM Suite** firm-wide; 200,000 onboarded users within 8 months; deployed across 230,000+ employees; self-reported 3–6 hours/week time savings, 30–40% efficiency gains, 10–20% productivity increase on coding teams; deck generation in ~30 seconds vs. hours manually; named American Banker 2025 Innovation of the Year (GenAI) ([jpmorganchase.com](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/about/technology/blog/llmsuite-ab-award), [thedigitalbanker.com](https://thedigitalbanker.com/jpmorgan-chases-llm-suite-drives-ai-transformation-across-the-enterprise/), [ciodive.com](https://www.ciodive.com/news/JPMorgan-Chase-LLM-Suite-generative-ai-employee-tool/726772/)). **Observed, high confidence** on deployment scale; **medium** on productivity figures (self-reported).
- *2024–2025* — Goldman Sachs rolls out **GS AI Assistant** firmwide (~46,000 employees, >50% adoption, target 100% by 2026); reports 15% reduction in post-release bug reports where used to modernize legacy code ([digitaldefynd.com](https://digitaldefynd.com/IQ/goldman-sachs-using-ai-case-study/), [opendatascience.com](https://opendatascience.com/goldman-sachs-rolls-out-ai-assistant-firmwide-to-boost-employee-productivity/)). **July 2025**: Goldman pilots Cognition **Devin**, becoming the first major bank to pilot a general autonomous coding agent (target 3–4× productivity over assistants), Devin supervised by engineers on legacy-modernization work ([cnbc.com 2025-07](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/11/goldman-sachs-autonomous-coder-pilot-marks-major-ai-milestone.html)). Citigroup deploys GitHub Copilot to ~40,000 developers with reported 2×–20× productivity on agentic tasks ([digitaldefynd.com](https://digitaldefynd.com/IQ/goldman-sachs-using-ai-case-study/)). **Observed, high confidence** on deployment; productivity labels self-reported.
- *2024–2026* — Bloomberg rolls out first generative-AI tool (AI-generated research summaries) to all Terminal users 2024–2025; new AI tool for Terminal announced for end-2025 ([institutionalinvestor.com](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsts3kce1udcfcy33z7k/portfolio/the-bloomberg-terminal-just-got-a-chatgpt-style-upgrade), [itbrew.com](https://www.itbrew.com/stories/2025/11/19/bloomberg-new-ai-tool-for-terminal)). Vendor-reported analyst query-time reduction of 20–30% in 2024 pilots ([belitsoft.com](https://belitsoft.com/bloomberggpt)). **Observed, medium confidence.**
- *2024–2025 regulatory* — FINRA issues Regulatory Notice 24-09 in **June 2024** reminding members of recordkeeping, customer-information protection, Reg BI, and risk-management obligations for generative AI ([finra.org 2026 ARO](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports/2026-finra-annual-regulatory-oversight-report/gen-ai), [wilmerhale.com](https://www.wilmerhale.com/-/media/files/shared_content/editorial/publications/wh_publications/client_alert_pdfs/20250218-finras-2025-annual-regulatory-oversight-report-focus-on-ai-other-emerging-risk-areas-and-best-practices.pdf)). FINRA's 2025 and 2026 Regulatory Oversight Reports list GenAI as a continuing and emerging trend; SEC Regulation S-P amendments (May 2024) tighten safeguards. Stated observation: firms proceed cautiously, default use case is internal summarization and information extraction, not client-facing advice ([finra.org 2026 ARO](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports/2026-finra-annual-regulatory-oversight-report/gen-ai)). **Observed, high confidence.**

**Example firms.** Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Bloomberg; plus scale-tier peers (BlackRock, BNY, BofA — not deep-analyzed here).

**Migration style.** **Phased continuous integration with strong gatekeeping.** Deployment shape is consistent: (1) an internal "ask" assistant grounded on the firm's own corpus (Morgan Stanley Assistant, JPMorgan LLM Suite, Goldman GS AI Assistant), then (2) a meeting/debrief agent (MS Debrief), then (3) specialized research or coding agents (AskResearchGPT, Goldman–Devin pilot, Citi–Copilot). Client-facing autonomous use cases lag internal ones by 12–24 months due to suitability, record-keeping, and Reg BI exposure. (**observed, high confidence**)

**Champion / CoE / vendor role.** A *Chief Data and Analytics Officer* or equivalent (Morgan Stanley's AI @ Morgan Stanley program, JPMorgan's firmwide Head of AI Transformation, Goldman's Engineering division) paired with *compliance-embedded AI governance committees* carries the program. Vendors are frontier-model providers (OpenAI for Morgan Stanley, Cognition for Goldman) with the firm retaining the RAG layer, evaluators, and governance. BloombergGPT illustrates the inverse pattern: a vendor tried to build an inside-out domain model and was out-performed by general-purpose GPT-4, so the vendor moved to integrate frontier models into its own surface rather than compete on training ([medium.com/arjun_shah](https://medium.com/@arjun_shah/bloombergs-10m-data-experiment-8c552ca5c212)). **Observed, high confidence**; **inferred, medium confidence** on the mechanism.

**Retooling velocity.** **Fast internally, slow externally.** Internal deploys followed capability releases by 3–12 months (GPT-4 release March 2023 → Morgan Stanley Assistant September 2023; GPT-4o / o1 → JPMorgan LLM Suite summer 2024). Client-facing workflows lag 12–24+ months because of Reg BI, suitability, and supervisory-review obligations. (**inferred, high confidence**)

**Rework cost.** **Medium.** The internal-first pattern absorbs most retooling cost inside a pre-existing IT operating model. The FINRA 2024 Reg Notice 24-09 added recordkeeping and customer-information-protection requirements that forced some firms to retrofit logging and retention, a non-trivial but bounded rework ([wilmerhale.com](https://www.wilmerhale.com/-/media/files/shared_content/editorial/publications/wh_publications/client_alert_pdfs/20250218-finras-2025-annual-regulatory-oversight-report-focus-on-ai-other-emerging-risk-areas-and-best-practices.pdf)). BloombergGPT represents the largest publicly visible **sunk-cost rework** in finance: a ~$10M domain training effort whose value was arguably eclipsed by general-purpose frontier models within 12 months ([medium.com/arjun_shah](https://medium.com/@arjun_shah/bloombergs-10m-data-experiment-8c552ca5c212)). **Observed / inferred, medium confidence.**

### 2.4 Software engineering — coding agents, code review, test generation

**Pre-AI workflow snapshot (2022).** The canonical developer workflow was: write code in an IDE; run tests locally; push to a branch; open a PR; code review by one or two engineers; CI runs; merge. Github Copilot (public GA June 2022) added inline completion; broader "agentic" behavior was not yet available. (**inferred from practice, high confidence**)

**Retooling events 2023–2026.**

- *2022–2024* — GitHub Copilot adoption scales across enterprises. Published research with ~4,800 developers reports 55% faster task completion; a three-company RCT (Microsoft, Accenture + one other) reports a 26.08% increase in completed tasks in the Copilot group ([secondtalent.com](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/github-copilot-statistics/), [resources.github.com](https://resources.github.com/learn/pathways/copilot/essentials/measuring-the-impact-of-github-copilot/)). A GitHub-published study (November 2024) finds code readability +3.62%, reliability +2.94%, maintainability +2.47%, conciseness +4.16%, approval-rate +5%, and a 29.1% rate of potential security weaknesses in Python generations ([secondtalent.com](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/github-copilot-statistics/)). **Observed, high confidence** on study design; **medium confidence** on generalization of self-reported percentages.
- *2023–2025* — Shopify scales Copilot to roughly half of its ~4,000 engineers by mid-2023; by March 2025 reports over 1,000,000 lines written with Copilot ([aibuilders.academy](https://aibuilders.academy/shopify-github-copilot/)). CEO Tobi Lütke publicly positions AI-tool usage as a promotion and hiring criterion (internal memo April 2025, widely circulated). **Observed, high confidence** on Shopify metrics.
- *2024–2025* — Cursor shifts enterprise developer workflow from Copilot-style completions to agentic, multi-file edits on a codebase-aware substrate. Stripe adoption reported to move from single digits to >80% rapidly; Shopify, Uber, Spotify also heavy users; Cursor reports >40,000 engineers across client firms using the tool; revenue crosses $1B ARR November 2025 and $2B ARR February 2026, with enterprise share ~45–60% by late 2025 ([sacra.com](https://sacra.com/research/cursor/), [opsera.ai](https://opsera.ai/blog/cursor-ai-adoption-trends-real-data-from-the-fastest-growing-coding-tool/), [programming-helper.com](https://www.programming-helper.com/tech/cursor-2026-ai-first-ide-composer-agents-python)). Cursor 2.0 / Composer 2 launched with multi-agent architecture for autonomous multi-file edits ([artezio.com](https://www.artezio.com/pressroom/blog/revolutionizes-architecture-proprietary/)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *July 2025* — Goldman Sachs pilots Cognition **Devin**, first general autonomous coding agent at a major bank, supervised by engineers ([cnbc.com 2025-07](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/11/goldman-sachs-autonomous-coder-pilot-marks-major-ai-milestone.html)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *July 2025 METR RCT* — randomized study of 16 experienced open-source developers over 246 real issues in high-star repositories finds that allowing AI use causes tasks to take **19% longer** (CI +2% to +39%), contradicting vendor productivity claims for experienced OSS developers on their own codebases; METR retools their experiment design February 2026 to mitigate participation bias ([metr.org 2025-07](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/), [metr.org 2026-02](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/), [arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089)). **Observed, high confidence** for the paper; **medium confidence** on external validity. This is the cleanest negative-productivity evidence in the entire A3 sample.
- *2024–2026 scale* — GitHub Copilot reaches 20M all-time users by July 2025, deployed across 90% of Fortune 100; 1.3M paid subscribers with 30% QoQ growth ([secondtalent.com](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/github-copilot-statistics/)). **Observed, high confidence.**

**Example firms.** Microsoft (self-use and study subject), Accenture, Shopify, Stripe, Uber, Spotify, Nvidia (endorsement), Goldman Sachs (Devin pilot), Citigroup (Copilot), Klarna (engineering-heavy usage).

**Migration style.** **Continuous integration with tool-substrate migrations.** The dominant shape is: Copilot-era inline completion (2022–2024) → IDE migration to Cursor / Windsurf / Claude Code (2024–2025) → agentic multi-file and autonomous-loop integration (2025–2026). No surveyed firm did a big-bang coder-replacement; Goldman's Devin pilot is explicitly supervised. Migration within engineering orgs is **tool-driven, not headcount-driven** — developers themselves swap IDEs at their own cadence, often with the firm licensing multiple assistants simultaneously. (**observed, high confidence**)

**Champion / CoE / vendor role.** **Developer-pull pattern, not CoE-push.** Unlike legal, medical, or finance, the strongest champions are individual senior engineers and platform-engineering teams; formal "AI CoE" plays a licensing / security / evaluation role but not a day-to-day workflow role. Vendors (GitHub, Cursor, Cognition, Anthropic, OpenAI) compete on substrate-level integration (codebase awareness, tool use, agent loops) rather than on advisory services. (**observed / inferred, high confidence**)

**Retooling velocity.** **Fast.** Release-to-production lag is the shortest of any domain — new model endpoints are integrated into Cursor and Claude Code within days, and developers adopt within weeks. Cursor's jump from single digits to >80% at Stripe over months of 2025 is indicative. (**observed, high confidence**)

**Rework cost.** **Medium, but disproportionately concentrated in review and evaluation.** The METR 19%-slower finding suggests that on high-complexity, experienced-developer tasks the "rework" manifests as invisible friction — reading generated code, debugging plausibly-wrong changes, context-repair — rather than headline rewrite cost. Security weaknesses in generated code (29.1% of Python samples per the GitHub study) accumulate as a long-tail maintenance tax. (**inferred from observed data, medium confidence**)

### 2.5 Consulting and knowledge services — analysis, decks, memos

**Pre-AI workflow snapshot (2022).** The canonical MBB workflow was: partner frames a question; associate does top-down hypothesis tree; junior consultants run interviews, build models, scrape comps, and draft slides on a precedent-PowerPoint substrate; editing cycles to partner-ready output took days per deck; a 4–6-person case team billed $1.5–3M per 10-week engagement. (**inferred from practice, high confidence**)

**Retooling events 2023–2026.**

- *July 2023* — McKinsey launches **Lilli** firmwide via QuantumBlack; by 2024–2025, 72% of employees use it; logs 500,000+ prompts/month; reclaims ~30% of research time ([mckinsey.com/quantumblack](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/how-we-help-clients), [managementconsulted.com](https://managementconsulted.com/quantumblack/)). QuantumBlack grows from 45 to 7,000+ technologists across 50 countries; 400+ GenAI build-outs across clients by Q2 2025, often replicating Lilli's RAG + expert-locator architecture ([mckinsey.com/quantumblack labs](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/labs)). **Observed, high confidence** on deployment; **medium confidence** on productivity.
- *March 2024* — BCG launches **Deckster** firmwide; by end-2024 used to create or edit 450,000+ slides; 6,000+ custom GPTs created by consultants; entire 32,000-person workforce trained, not only a subset ([computerworld.com](https://www.computerworld.com/article/3491334/bcg-execs-ai-across-the-company-increased-productivity-employee-joy.html), [bcg.com genai-transforming](https://www.bcg.com/capabilities/artificial-intelligence/generative-ai)). BCG hires 1,000 additional staff in 2024 specifically for AI services; 2024 AI-advisory revenue ~$2.7B of $13.5B total (~20%) ([brainforge.ai](https://www.brainforge.ai/blog/how-big-consulting-firms-profit-massively-from-ai-consulting), [bcg.com executive perspectives](https://media-publications.bcg.com/BCG-Executive-Perspectives-Unlocking-Impact-from-AI-HR-EP1-30July2024.pdf)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *September 2023 BCG–Harvard–Wharton study* — 758 consultants randomized across GPT-4 vs. control on 18 representative consulting tasks; GenAI-augmented consultants achieved 86% of a data-scientist benchmark on tasks outside their skillset (a +49 percentage-point improvement over controls) and completed tasks ~10% faster ([bcg.com press 2024-09-05](https://www.bcg.com/press/5september2024-generative-ai-knowledge-workers-consultants)). **Observed, high confidence** — peer-reviewed RCT. This is the strongest consulting-domain evidence for capability expansion; it also frames "jagged frontier" risk — AI helps on some tasks and hurts on others.
- *March 2025* — Deloitte unveils **Zora AI**, an agentic platform built on NVIDIA AI, targeted at finance, HR, supply chain, procurement, sales/marketing, and customer service; implementations in weeks to months; internal goal of deploying to thousands of users by end-2025 and achieving ~25% cost reduction / ~40% productivity increase in finance ([deloitte.com US](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/services/consulting/services/zora-generative-ai-agent.html), [deloitte.com Zora unveil](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/deloitte-unveils-zora-ai-agentic-ai-for-tomorrows-workforce.html), [cio.com](https://www.cio.com/article/3848252/deloitte-unveils-agentic-ai-platform.html)). Deloitte announces Zora integrations with Oracle OCI (Oct 2025), SAP Joule/BTP (May 2025), HPE Private Cloud AI (March 2025) ([deloitte.com Oracle](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/deloitte-and-oracle-accelerate-agentic-ai-with-zora-ai.html), [deloitte.com SAP](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/deloitte-to-integrate-zora-ai-with-sap-joule-agents.html)). **Observed, high confidence** on launch; **medium confidence** on stated productivity targets.
- *2023–2026* — PwC commits US$1B over 3 years to AI capability expansion and trains 65,000+ US staff on AI tools ([pwc.com US $1B](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/about-us/newsroom/press-releases/pwc-us-makes-billion-investment-in-ai-capabilities.html)). PwC CEE implements Harvey across tax, legal, HR practices ([cee.pwc.com](https://cee.pwc.com/pwc-cee-tax-legal-and-people-practice-embraces-the-ai-work-revolution-by-implementing-the-harvey-legal-ai-platform.html)). Accenture plans to reach 80,000 data/AI professionals by 2026 ([brainforge.ai](https://www.brainforge.ai/blog/how-big-consulting-firms-profit-massively-from-ai-consulting)). **Observed, high confidence.**
- *2024–2025 failure signal* — Gartner: 30% of GenAI projects abandoned after POC by end-2025. MIT Sloan 2025: 95% of GenAI pilots fail to scale to production. McKinsey Global AI Survey Nov 2025: 88% of organizations use AI in at least one function, only 39% see any EBIT impact; BCG Oct 2024 / Sept 2025: 74% → 60% of companies report no material value from AI. 42% of organizations report abandoning AI initiatives in 2025, up from 17% in 2024 ([astrafy.io](https://astrafy.io/the-hub/blog/technical/scaling-ai-from-pilot-purgatory-why-only-33-reach-production-and-how-to-beat-the-odds), [linkedin.com/in/stephenbklein](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stephenbklein_the-reason-95-of-genai-pilots-fail-has-nothing-activity-7363732361357783041-Lkkj)). **Observed, medium confidence** — figures from multiple syntheses rather than one primary survey, but directionally consistent.
- *2024–2025* — Klarna reverses its public "AI can do the work of 700 agents" stance (Feb 2024 claim) and begins rehiring human customer-service agents in mid-2025, citing lower output quality; handled as hybrid AI-triage + human-resolution ([customerexperiencedive.com](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/), [entrepreneur.com](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396), [mlq.ai/news](https://mlq.ai/news/klarna-ceo-admits-aggressive-ai-job-cuts-went-too-far-starts-hiring-again-after-us-ipo/)). **Observed, high confidence.** Not strictly a consulting firm, but included because consulting-adjacent knowledge-services firms cited Klarna as a template in 2023–2024, making the reversal a load-bearing negative case.

**Example firms.** McKinsey (Lilli / QuantumBlack), BCG (Deckster, GENE, BCG X), Bain (Vector and internal tools), Deloitte (Zora), Accenture (Managed Services AI), PwC (Harvey alliance), EY.

**Migration style.** **Big-bang training + continuous tool rollout, uniquely among the five domains.** BCG and McKinsey uniquely trained *the entire workforce* on GenAI rather than a pilot subset, while continuously adding tools (Lilli → Deckster → custom GPTs → agentic platforms like Zora). The firm's billable-human product and the tool are co-evolved. (**observed, high confidence**)

**Champion / CoE / vendor role.** **Internal CoE + internal vendor.** QuantumBlack, BCG X, Deloitte AI Institute, and Accenture's Gen AI Studio simultaneously serve internal employees and sell external services — an unusual self-consuming CoE. External vendors (OpenAI, Anthropic, NVIDIA) are substrate providers; the consulting firm's CoE owns the product layer. (**observed, high confidence**)

**Retooling velocity.** **Fast internally, very fast externally.** Internal consultant tools follow capability releases within weeks to months (GPT-4 → Lilli July 2023, ~4 months). External client-facing build-outs (400+ at McKinsey by Q2 2025) scale at the rate the firm can hire (BCG +1,000 AI staff 2024; Accenture target 80,000 by 2026). (**observed, high confidence**)

**Rework cost.** **High at client level, medium internally.** The 95% pilot-failure / 60% no-material-value / 42% abandonment figures imply the *client-side* rework cost of consulting AI engagements is high. Internal firm tooling rework is absorbed as part of the CoE's R&D budget (a fixed cost amortized across thousands of engagements). Klarna's reversal is the most visible single example of a consulting-prescribed "AI replaces humans" playbook being reworked. (**inferred, medium confidence**)

## 4. Transferable patterns

All confidence labels below refer to the generalization across the five domains studied, not within any single domain.

**T1. Ground-and-gate before autonomy.** Every domain that reached firm-scale deployment first built a *grounded retrieval layer* on top of the firm's own corpus (ContractMatrix, AI @ Morgan Stanley Assistant, JPMorgan LLM Suite, Lilli, CoCounsel) before any agentic or multi-step autonomy was released. The ground-and-gate architecture is then *extended* with agentic capabilities on the same substrate rather than replaced. (**high confidence**)

**T2. Continuous integration dominates over big-bang.** In four of five domains, continuous incremental integration — beta → named pilot → scoped rollout → adjacent capability — is the overwhelmingly dominant migration style. The only exception is consulting's *big-bang workforce training*, which still pairs with continuous tool rollout. (**high confidence**)

**T3. Internal first, external second.** Every regulated or high-stakes domain deployed to internal employees on internal corpora before touching a client-facing or patient-facing artifact. Finance lags the internal-to-external transition by 12–24 months; medical by FDA-cycle years; legal by the firm's malpractice-risk appetite. (**high confidence**)

**T4. The vendor-partnered innovation unit is the stable organizational form.** Across legal (A&O MIG + Harvey), medical (CMIO + Epic + Abridge/Nuance/Nabla), finance (CDAO + OpenAI/Cognition), and consulting (internal CoE + OpenAI/Anthropic/NVIDIA), the durable retooling unit is a *small internal innovation team co-developing with one or two anchor vendors*, not a big central-IT rollout and not a standalone research lab. Software engineering is the exception — see D1 below. (**high confidence**)

**T5. Independent RCTs blunt vendor productivity claims.** In the three domains where rigorous randomized studies exist — software engineering (METR –19% on complex tasks), ambient clinical scribes (UCLA RCT: Nabla –9.5% time-in-note, DAX no significant change), consulting (BCG–Harvard–Wharton: +10% speed, +49pp on out-of-skillset tasks) — the independent numbers are systematically smaller (or in METR's case, opposite-signed) than vendor self-reports. A transferable discipline is to anchor rollout decisions on the RCT rather than the vendor case study. (**high confidence**)

**T6. Capability-released-to-workflow-changed lag is domain-specific, not firm-specific.** Velocity clusters by domain (software < consulting ≤ finance internal < legal < medical < finance external ≤ imaging regulatory) more than by firm size or capital availability. A top-10 law firm and a top-50 law firm are closer in retooling cadence than a top-10 law firm is to a top-10 bank. (**medium confidence**)

**T7. The retooling trigger is a capability phase change, not an incremental version bump.** Domains accelerated retooling around four phase changes: (1) long-context + RAG maturation late 2023; (2) GPT-4o / multimodal mid-2024; (3) o1-class reasoning late 2024; (4) agent-substrate tooling (Devin, Cursor Composer, Dragon Copilot agentic layer, A&O agentic tools) 2025. Incremental model updates between these phase changes did not produce structural workflow change. (**medium confidence**)

## 5. Domain-locked patterns

**D1. Developer-pull tool migration (software only).** Only software engineering has the property that individual developers can change the primary AI tool (Copilot → Cursor → Claude Code) at personal cadence without coordination cost. Legal, medical, finance, and consulting are all *platform-locked* — the firm, the EHR, or the governance committee must sanction a change because the artifacts pass into regulated or client-facing systems. This is the clearest single domain-locked pattern. (**high confidence**)

**D2. Hallucination-sanction feedback loop (legal only).** Only legal has a public sanction mechanism (Rule 11, state bar rules, malpractice liability) that converts each individual attorney's AI mistake into case-law-visible precedent. The AI-Hallucination-Cases database with 850+ filings and near-daily new cases in late 2025 is a unique feedback signal ([damiencharlotin.com](https://www.damiencharlotin.com/hallucinations/)). Equivalent public-accountability feedback does not exist at comparable density in medical, finance, software, or consulting. (**high confidence**)

**D3. EHR-integration gatekeeping (medical only).** In medicine the Epic / Oracle Health integration layer is the chokepoint. Abridge becoming Epic's first "Pal" ([abridge.com](https://www.abridge.com/press-release/abridge-becomes-epics-first-pal-bringing-generative-ai-to-more-providers-and-patients)) and Nuance embedding DAX Copilot into Epic's workflows ([prnewswire.com 2024-01](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nuance-announces-general-availability-of-dax-copilot-embedded-in-epic-transforming-healthcare-experiences-with-automated-clinical-documentation-302037590.html)) define retooling velocity ceilings that no other domain has. No equivalent single-platform gatekeeper exists in legal (mixed iManage / NetDocuments / direct-Word use), finance (mixed Bloomberg / FactSet / internal platforms), consulting (mixed PowerPoint / internal decks), or software (mixed IDEs). (**high confidence**)

**D4. Client-facing-authority constraint (finance wealth management only).** In wealth management the registered-representative's suitability obligation means that AI recommendations to clients require specific recordkeeping and supervisor review. Morgan Stanley Debrief's design — AI drafts a note, advisor edits and sends — directly encodes this constraint ([morganstanley.com Debrief launch](https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/ai-at-morgan-stanley-debrief-launch)). The same workflow shape is unnecessary in consulting (partner signs deliverables), ambiguous in software (code review gates it), and formally absent in medical scribe use (the physician always signs the note). (**medium confidence**)

**D5. Self-consuming consulting CoE (consulting only).** QuantumBlack, BCG X, Deloitte AI, and Accenture Gen AI Studio *simultaneously* retool the firm's consultants and sell the same retooling as client services. No other domain has a CoE that is also the firm's external product. This dual-use role makes the consulting CoE more experimental and more willing to big-bang-train than a legal or medical innovation unit would be. (**medium confidence**)

**D6. Regulatory predicate cost (medical imaging only).** A substantively updated imaging AI model may require 510(k) predicate reanalysis. This forces medical imaging onto a retooling cadence measured in months-to-years per model, not days-to-weeks as in software. (**high confidence**)

**D7. Trust-calibrated individualized use (weak generalization, primarily legal + medical).** In legal and medical, the individual professional — attorney, physician — is the liability-bearing user, and workflow design accommodates their personal trust calibration (attorney verifies citations, physician signs note). In finance, software, and consulting, trust is partly transferred to a supervisor or reviewer. This partial transfer is what permits software's developer-pull pattern but not legal's or medical's. (**medium confidence**)

## 6. Open questions and negative evidence

**Gaps in the sample.**

- Japanese and Korean adopters in all five domains: the public-disclosure cadence differs from US/UK, and the evidence base available on 2026-04-22 is thin. Flagging for M1/M2 follow-up.
- Mid-market firms (AmLaw 51–200, mid-size community hospitals, sub-$50B-AUM asset managers, Tier-2 consulting): the data above is heavily top-of-market.
- Underwriting-specific retooling (mortgage, P&C insurance, credit) was listed in the plan seed but surfaces thinly; I did not find a top-tier case with the same reporting density as Morgan Stanley Debrief or JPMorgan LLM Suite at the time of writing.
- **Kirkland & Ellis** is an explicit plan seed; public disclosure is limited to BTI awards and AI-practice-area press rather than a workflow case. Flagging as an open item.

**Negative evidence worth preserving.**

- **METR's 19%-slower RCT** is the clearest published negative productivity finding in any of the five domains. It affects confidence in vendor self-reports for software engineering. (**high confidence**)
- **BloombergGPT** underperformed general-purpose GPT-4 on most financial benchmarks shortly after release, meaning a ~$10M domain-trained LLM effort was overtaken within 12 months. (**high confidence**)
- **30–95% pilot-failure rates and 42% initiative abandonment in 2025** ([astrafy.io](https://astrafy.io/the-hub/blog/technical/scaling-ai-from-pilot-purgatory-why-only-33-reach-production-and-how-to-beat-the-odds)) dampen the headline consulting-firm success narrative.
- **Klarna's reversal** from "700 agents of work" in February 2024 to rehiring human agents in 2025 is a load-bearing counter-example for full-autonomy consulting prescriptions.
- **Legal hallucination sanctions** at near-daily cadence in late 2025 indicate the *verification tax* on legal AI is real, sometimes exceeding the drafting-time savings on the same document.

**Open questions for further work.**

- What is the *steady-state* ratio of time saved on drafting to time added on verification, by domain? The only numbers with independent anchoring are Nabla's 9.5% time-in-note reduction and METR's –19% on complex OSS tasks; the rest are firm-reported.
- For the vendor-partnered innovation-unit pattern (T4), what happens when the anchor vendor changes strategy (Microsoft absorbing Nuance DAX into Dragon Copilot; GE HealthCare's six-vendor imaging acquisition)? The retooling cost of vendor consolidation is not yet visible in the data.
- Is the consulting "self-consuming CoE" stable, or does client disappointment (60% no-material-value) erode it? Flagging for O1 / O3 cross-reference.
- For A1 / A2: which capability phase changes (T7) triggered retooling in *which* domains first? The ordering — software first, consulting next, finance internal, legal, medical — has not been formally timestamped.

## 7. Feedback to the three-pillar adaptability frame

- **Workflow retooling is load-bearing on Pillar 1.** The dominant AI-usage pattern is not "which model" but "which *workflow architecture*." Ground-and-gate-then-extend (T1) and internal-first-then-external (T3) are the two most generalizable AI-usage design heuristics across domains. A1's model-transition framework should treat workflow architecture, not the model version, as the unit of change.
- **Domain-locked constraints live in Pillar 2.** D1 (developer-pull), D2 (legal sanctions), D3 (EHR gatekeeping), D4 (wealth-management suitability), D5 (consulting self-consuming CoE) are organizational-structure facts, not AI-usage facts. They should populate O1's "substantive vs. public-relations restructuring" distinction and O2's role-life-cycle model — e.g., CMIO is durable; "prompt engineer" is transient; Harvey co-development partner is new and durable; Devin supervisor is new and possibly transient.
- **Retooling velocity is the cross-pillar coupling variable.** Software's fast cadence is enabled by Pillar 3 flexibility (developer-pull on open IDEs), Pillar 2 permission (individual engineers can swap tools), and Pillar 1 substrate compatibility (tool-agnostic model APIs). Medical's slow cadence is the composition of Pillar 3 rigidity (EHR lock-in), Pillar 2 structure (CMIO approval gate), and Pillar 1 regulatory overlay (FDA 510(k) predicate cost). This compositionality is exactly the kind of coupling M1's systems-dynamics map should model: each domain is a different *parameterization* of the same three-pillar graph.
- **Negative evidence should be promoted, not hidden.** METR's –19%, BloombergGPT's GPT-4-outperformed finding, the 42% abandonment rate, and Klarna's reversal are precisely the kind of anti-hype signals the adaptability frame depends on to avoid overcorrection. M2's indicator set should include an *adaptation-to-overcorrection ratio* (already in the research plan) and an *independent-RCT-to-vendor-claim ratio*.
- **Trust calibration is a prerequisite for continuous integration.** The legal hallucination-sanction loop (D2) and the ambient-scribe physician-signature loop (D7) both show that the individual user's trust-calibrated verification is what makes continuous integration safe. This connects to the reasoning-trust-verification track called out in the plan's Section 4: trust calibration is not an add-on but a *structural enabler* of the dominant migration style across four of five domains.
- **M1/M2 note.** A testable M1 feedback loop: "retooling velocity = f(vendor-partner count, regulatory gate depth, user liability concentration)" — software, consulting, and finance-internal high on the first, medium on the second, low on the third; medical and legal inverted. A testable M2 indicator: *time-from-capability-phase-change-to-production-workflow-redesign*, per T7, anchored to four 2023–2025 phase dates and measurable by firm.
